In short,

I am a postdoctoral research fellow in the Division of Applied Mathematics at Brown University, working in the group of Prof. Kavita Ramanan. Before joining Brown, I completed my PhD in probability theory at Eindhoven University of Technology under the supervision of Prof. Remco van der Hofstad and Dr. Rajat Subhra Hazra. My doctoral thesis is titled "Stochastic Processes on Preferential Attachment Models".

My work is about networks: the webs of connections behind social media, the internet, power grids and the spread of diseases. I study how individuals in such a network (people, computers or particles) influence their neighbours, and how these small, local exchanges add up to large-scale effects, like a rumour going viral, a blackout spreading through a city or an epidemic taking off. The twist is that these networks are themselves random and keep changing over time, so I use probability theory to make sense of them.

Away from the blackboard, I enjoy table tennis and chess, and football, where my contributions remain largely stochastic. I am an avid reader of comics and Bengali storybooks, and I travel whenever I can, with the modest long-term goal of seeing the whole world. Wherever I go, I make a point of trying the local specialities. As a foodie, I consider this essential fieldwork, sampled generously and, whenever possible, with replacement.
